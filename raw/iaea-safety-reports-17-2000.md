## Overview

This IAEA Safety Report reviews a large number of historical radiotherapy accidents to serve as a learning resource and checklist for radiation therapy facilities worldwide. Its core purpose is to help facilities test their own vulnerability to potential accidents, and to provide a basis for improving safety practices before accidents occur.

---

## Purpose

1. Serve as a **review of historical events** that can function as a checklist against which to test a facility's vulnerability to potential accidents.
2. **Encourage a questioning and learning attitude** among radiotherapy professionals.
3. Support the adoption of **proactive safety measures** to prevent accidents before they happen.
4. Guide preparation for **mitigation of consequences** if accidents do occur.

---

## Historical Accident Case Studies

The report draws from documented major radiotherapy accidents investigated by the IAEA, including incidents in:
- **San José, Costa Rica** — overexposure accident
- **Panama** — overexposure resulting in patient deaths, related to treatment planning system errors
- **Bialystok, Poland** — overexposure accident

These cases illustrate how systemic failures — rather than single errors — lead to major accidents.

---

## Key Lessons from Accident Analysis

- Most accidents involve **multiple contributing failures** rather than a single isolated error.
- **Equipment malfunctions** alone rarely cause accidents; human and procedural failures are almost always also present.
- **Independent verification** and **redundant checks** are the most effective accident-prevention layers.
- Absence of a safety culture (where errors are not reported for fear of blame) is a recurring systemic risk factor.

---

## Key Recommendations

1. **Independent calibration:** Calibration of radiotherapy equipment must be independently verified — not solely by the operator who set it up.
2. **Formalized clinical checklists:** Standardized, documented checklists at each critical step of the treatment chain.
3. **Proactive safety management systems:** Adoption of the SEPAR framework (Safety Evaluation of Radiation Therapy) as a structured approach to safety management.
4. **Multi-layer safety:** Accidents can be prevented by applying **several safety layers of preventive actions** (defense in depth).
5. **Learning from prior incidents:** Systematic review of incident reports — both internal and external — as an ongoing safety practice.

---

## SEPAR Framework

- **S**afety **E**valuation of **R**adiation therapy **A**pplied to **R**outine practice.
- Structured framework for systematic safety evaluation within radiotherapy departments.
- Emphasizes proactive identification of vulnerabilities before incidents occur.
