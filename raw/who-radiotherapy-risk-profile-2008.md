## Overview

The WHO Radiotherapy Risk Profile is the only source document that bases its safety recommendations primarily on **evidence from actual events and near-misses** — rather than theoretical risk frameworks. It reviews reports from peer-reviewed and gray literature, performs root cause analysis on real incidents, and deduces safety measures that would have reduced the probability of each event occurring.

---

## Scope and Methodology

- Reviewed a large body of international clinical incident reports.
- Analyzed **3,125 errors** and **4,616 quasi-errors (near-misses)**.
- Performed root cause analysis on documented radiotherapy accidents worldwide.
- Synthesized international clinical data to identify the most common causes of accidental over- or under-exposures.

## Key Findings

- **Most common contributing factor:** Dosimetry-related errors are the most frequently occurring category.
- Errors occur across the entire treatment chain — not only in physics/dosimetry but also in clinical decision-making, communication, and handovers.
- **Systematic patterns:** Most accidents share common root causes: inadequate independent verification, poor communication at handovers, insufficient checklists, and failure to detect equipment malfunctions.

---

## Key Recommendations

1. **Comprehensive Delivery Quality Assurance (DQA):** Introduction of a DQA program in every radiotherapy practice is described as **mandatory** to reduce the number and/or severity of incidents.
2. **Holistic QA scope:** Quality assurance should not be confined to physical and technical aspects only — it should encompass all activities in a radiation oncology centre from the moment a patient enters until the time they leave, and continue throughout follow-up.
3. **Double-checks:** Independent verification of critical parameters (doses, field sizes, patient identity) at every key stage.
4. **Independent dosimetry validation:** Secondary dose calculations as a mandatory check.
5. **Robust communication at handovers:** Standardized communication protocols between team members, especially at shift changes and care transitions.

---

## Significance

- Evidence-based foundation: unlike TG-100 (which is proactive/prospective), the WHO profile is retrospective — grounded in what actually went wrong.
- Serves as an authoritative reference for international radiotherapy safety standards.
- Widely cited alongside IAEA safety reports as part of the foundational patient safety literature in radiation oncology.
