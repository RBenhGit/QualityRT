# Regulatory Framework for Radiation Therapy in the USA

## Overview

Regulatory authority over radiation therapy in the United States is distributed across multiple federal and state agencies. No single body governs all aspects of RT safety; instead, authority is divided between the NRC (or Agreement States), FDA, and state radiation control programs, with voluntary accreditation bodies providing complementary oversight.

---

## Primary Federal Regulators

### Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC)
- Regulates **medical use of radioactive materials** (byproduct materials) — isotopes used in brachytherapy, I-131 therapy, HDR sources
- Has regulated medical radioactive materials since 1946
- Goals: ensure radioactive materials are used properly to protect patients, workers, the public, and environment
- **Backgrounder URL:** https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/fact-sheets/med-use-radioactive-materials
- **Key tools:** licensing facilities, authorizing individuals, inspections, investigations, enforcement

### Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
- Regulates approval of **radiation-producing machines** (LINACs, CT simulators) and radiopharmaceuticals
- However, once approved, **states** (not FDA) regulate actual use
- NRC and FDA coordinate through a **Memorandum of Understanding**; conduct joint inspections on device failures and medical errors

---

## The Agreement States System

### How It Works
- NRC shares regulatory authority with **Agreement States** — states that have signed agreements with the NRC
- Once signed, the state takes over regulatory authority (licensing, inspection, enforcement) over radioactive materials within its borders
- NRC retains oversight: periodically reviews state radiation control programs to ensure continued adequacy

### Coverage
- Most U.S. states are Agreement States
- Non-Agreement States, D.C., Puerto Rico, and U.S. territories are regulated directly by the NRC
- This creates the possibility of **regulatory variation**: requirements differ by state

### CRCPD (Conference of Radiation Control Program Directors)
- Organization of state radiation control programs
- Develops **Suggested State Regulations (SSRs)** to promote consistency across Agreement States
- URL: https://www.nrc.gov/materials/toolboxes/regulation/crcpd-suggestions

---

## State Radiation Regulation

- Radiation-producing devices (LINACs, simulators) are regulated at the **state level** by 50 different state regulatory agencies
- This creates **inconsistency**: a lack of uniformity in radiation safety regulations across states
- External beam radiotherapy using LINACs (no radioactive sources) falls primarily under **state, not NRC, jurisdiction**
- Each state's radiation control program sets licensing, inspection, and QA requirements for RT facilities using LINACs

---

## Accreditation and Professional Society Standards (Complementary)

Although not regulatory bodies, these organizations effectively set standards of practice:

### ACR (American College of Radiology) / ASTRO
- **ACR-ASTRO Practice Parameters** define standards for RT procedures
- Accreditation programs require documented QA programs, staffing qualifications, and equipment standards

### The Joint Commission (TJC)
- Accredits hospitals; includes radiation oncology departments
- Requires sentinel event reporting and root cause analysis after serious adverse events

### CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services)
- Ties reimbursement to quality and safety requirements
- Conditions of Participation (CoPs) require hospitals to maintain patient safety programs

---

## Mandatory Incident Reporting

### NRC Medical Event Reporting
- NRC regulations (10 CFR 35.3045) require **mandatory reporting of medical events** involving radioactive materials:
  - Wrong patient
  - Wrong treatment site
  - Wrong radionuclide
  - Dose >20% above or below prescribed
  - Doses causing unintended damage
- Reports go to NRC and must be shared with referring physician and patient

### State Reporting
- Agreement States have equivalent reporting requirements, often mirroring NRC rules
- External beam RT events (LINAC-based) reported to state radiation control programs

---

## Key Regulatory Gap

- **No federal mandate exists** for RT incident reporting equivalent to PSQIA protections for RO-ILS
- Mandatory NRC/state reports are public record — no federal privilege protection (unlike RO-ILS under PSQIA)
- This creates a two-track system: mandatory public reports (NRC/state) vs. voluntary protected reports (RO-ILS)
