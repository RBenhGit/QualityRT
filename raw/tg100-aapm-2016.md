## Overview

The TG-100 report represents a landmark paradigm shift in radiotherapy quality management: moving away from prescriptive machine-only checking toward proactive, workflow-based risk analysis. The task group selected three industrial engineering risk assessment tools because of their widespread acceptance in high-reliability industries.

---

## Three Core Risk Analysis Tools

### 1. Process Mapping
- Detailing all clinical activities step-by-step, from patient consult through treatment delivery.
- Identifies hand-offs between team members, which are a major source of errors.
- Enables visualization of the entire treatment workflow including decision points.

### 2. Failure Modes and Effects Analysis (FMEA)
- Proactive evaluation of what can fail at each step of a clinical process.
- Each failure mode is scored on three dimensions:
  - **Severity (S):** How harmful is the failure if it occurs?
  - **Occurrence (O):** How often does this failure occur?
  - **Detectability (D):** How easily can the failure be caught before it harms the patient?
- **Risk Priority Number (RPN):** Calculated as RPN = S × O × D
- RPN is used to prioritize which failure modes require the most urgent mitigation.
- Intensity Modulated Radiation Therapy (IMRT) is used as a primary example application in the report.
- Scale is typically 1–10 for each dimension; RPN ranges from 1–1,000.

### 3. Fault Tree Analysis (FTA)
- Systematically analyzes the root causes of major clinical failures.
- Works top-down: starts from a known adverse outcome and traces back to root causes.
- Complements FMEA (which is bottom-up: starts from individual failure modes).

## Key Findings and Context

- Modern radiotherapy involves complex, multistep decision-making with significant hand-offs between team members.
- Traditional equipment-focused QC, while important, fails to address process-related risks.
- RO-ILS database (>10,000 events) shows incident distribution:
  - **Treatment Planning:** 30% of reported incidents
  - **Treatment Delivery:** 26% of reported incidents
- Common planning failures: hand-offs between team members, target/ROI contouring errors, ambiguous physician instructions, plan revisions, rushed timelines.
- Common delivery failures: patient identification errors, isocenter alignment issues, HDR brachytherapy errors.

---

## Design Philosophy

- **Customization:** QM plans should be tailored to specific clinical resources and technology, not applied as generic checklists.
- **Risk-based prioritization:** Focus resources on highest-RPN processes.
- **Team-based approach:** The entire clinical team (receptionists, nurses, dosimetrists, radiation oncologists, radiation therapists, medical physicists) contributes to workflow safety.

---

## Implementation Barriers

- TG-100 was not developed as a collaborative effort with physician, radiation therapist, and dosimetrist societies — leading to poor awareness outside medical physics.
- Organizational culture resistance to addressing workflow inefficiencies.
- Lack of pervasive safety cultures in healthcare organizations.

## Significance

- Published in *Medical Physics*, the flagship journal of AAPM.
- Positioned medical physicists as analytical leaders for clinical process improvement.
- Basis for many subsequent FMEA implementation studies in radiotherapy departments worldwide.
