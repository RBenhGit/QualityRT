# RO-ILS: Radiation Oncology Incident Learning System

## Bibliographic Info

- **Full Name:** RO-ILS: Radiation Oncology Incident Learning System
- **Sponsoring Bodies:** American Society for Radiation Oncology (ASTRO) & American Association of Physicists in Medicine (AAPM)
- **Operations Partner:** Clarity Patient Safety Organization (PSO) — a federally recognized PSO
- **URL:** https://www.astro.org/practice-support/quality-and-safety/ro-ils
- **Launch:** Part of ASTRO's Target Safely patient protection initiative, established 2010

---

## Overview

RO-ILS is the only medical specialty society-sponsored radiation oncology incident learning system in the United States. It provides a secure, online portal for radiation oncology centers to document, track, analyze, and trend patient safety-related incidents in a non-punitive environment, then share aggregate data nationally to enable shared learning.

---

## Purpose and Mission

- Facilitate safer and higher-quality care in radiation oncology.
- Provide a mechanism for shared learning in a secure and non-punitive environment.
- Enable practices to document near-misses and safety incidents, and learn from national aggregate patterns.

---

## How It Works

1. Radiation oncology centers enroll (no enrollment fee).
2. Staff log incidents or near-misses through a secure online portal.
3. Data is submitted to Clarity PSO for safekeeping under federal legal protections.
4. The Radiation Oncology Healthcare Advisory Council (RO-HAC) reviews aggregate national data.
5. RO-HAC publishes case studies, safety notices, and quarterly benchmarking reports.
6. Institutions receive quarterly institution-specific benchmarking reports comparing their data to national trends.

---

## Legal Protections

- Operates under the **Patient Safety and Quality Improvement Act (PSQIA) of 2005**.
- Reports submitted to a PSO are granted **federal confidentiality and privilege protections**.
- Data cannot be used in medical malpractice litigation.
- This legal framework enables the "just culture" of non-punitive reporting.

---

## Key Features

- **Non-punitive reporting:** Staff can log incidents without fear of blame or legal consequences.
- **No cost:** No enrollment fees for radiation therapy centers.
- **National database access:** Aggregate reports on equipment, techniques, and dosimetric severity classifications.
- **MOC Credit:** Qualifies as MOC Part 4 PQI (Practice Quality Improvement) activity for ABR (American Board of Radiology) maintenance of certification.
- **RO-HAC oversight:** 8-member expert council (radiation oncologists, physicists, administrators, dosimetrists) reviews all incoming data.

---

## Radiation Oncology Healthcare Advisory Council (RO-HAC)

- Composition: Radiation oncologists, medical physicists, administrators, and dosimetrists.
- Responsibilities:
  - Review incoming incident data from across the country.
  - Produce participant alerts when urgent safety patterns emerge.
  - Publish case studies and periodic safety reports for the radiation oncology community.

---

## Data Scale

- The RO-ILS database contains over 10,000 reported events.
- Incident distribution (per analysis published in JACM 2019):
  - Treatment Planning: ~30% of incidents
  - Treatment Delivery: ~26% of incidents

---

## Relationship to TG-100

- RO-ILS provides the real-world incident data that validates TG-100's risk analysis findings.
- TG-100's FMEA approach can be informed by RO-ILS aggregate data to prioritize which failure modes have the highest occurrence rates nationally.
