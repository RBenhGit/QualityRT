# IAEA Safety Culture Traits — Summary

**Date:** 2026-05-31
**Source:** IAEA Radiation Safety Culture Trait Talks Handbook (radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks.pdf)

## The 10 IAEA Safety Culture Traits

1. **Individual Responsibility** — Every person takes personal responsibility for safety — for patients, coworkers, and the public — as a genuine commitment, not merely compliance with orders.

2. **Questioning Attitude** — Never accept anomalies at face value; actively challenge assumptions and stop when something doesn't look right.

3. **Effective Safety Communication** — Safety information flows freely in all directions — up, down, and across the organization.

4. **Leadership Responsibility** — Leaders visibly prioritize safety over cost and schedule, and set the example through their daily decisions and presence.

5. **Decision-Making** — Safety decisions are systematic and thorough, with a bias toward conservative choices when facing uncertainty.

6. **Respectful Work Environment** — Trust and mutual respect allow people to voice disagreement and raise concerns without fear.

7. **Continuous Learning** — The organization actively mines its own experience and others' for lessons, and acts on them.

8. **Problem Identification and Resolution** — Issues are reported early, root-caused rigorously, and fixed — not buried or patched.

9. **Environment for Raising Concerns** — Staff can report safety problems through any channel without fear of retaliation.

10. **Work Processes** — Work is planned, documented, and executed to procedure — safety is built into how things are done, not added on top.
