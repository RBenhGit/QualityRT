# FMEA in Radiotherapy

**Type:** Topic
**Status:** developing
**Last updated:** 2026-05-24
**Sources:** [radiotherapy-sources]

## Summary
Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA) is the primary proactive risk analysis tool in radiotherapy quality management, formalized by the AAPM TG-100 report (2016). Each potential failure in the clinical workflow is scored on three axes — Severity (S), Occurrence (O), and Detectability (D) — to yield a Risk Priority Number (RPN), directing mitigation resources where risk is highest.

## Key Facts / Claims
- FMEA evaluates what can fail in a clinical workflow, scored on: Severity (S), Occurrence (O), and Detectability (D) [radiotherapy-sources]
- Risk Priority Number formula: **RPN = S × O × D** [radiotherapy-sources]
- Fault Tree Analysis (FTA) is a complementary method for tracing root causes of major failures in a top-down, logical structure [radiotherapy-sources]
- Process Mapping — detailing all clinical activities step-by-step from patient consult to treatment delivery — is a prerequisite for FMEA in the TG-100 framework [radiotherapy-sources]
- Highest-RPN workflow stages identified in practice: contouring, treatment planning, data transfer (DICOM), and patient positioning [radiotherapy-sources]
- Mitigation strategies: standardized checklist templates, automated electronic data transfers, and robust physical setup protocols [radiotherapy-sources]
- TG-100 advocates designing customized QM plans based on specific department resources and technology, not generic checklists [radiotherapy-sources]
