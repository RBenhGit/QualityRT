# AAPM Task Group 100 (TG-100)

**Type:** Topic
**Status:** developing
**Last updated:** 2026-05-31
**Sources:** [tg100-aapm-2016]

## Summary

The AAPM Task Group 100 report (Huq et al., *Medical Physics*, July 2016) is the landmark publication that shifted radiotherapy quality management from prescriptive machine-only checking toward proactive, workflow-based risk analysis. It introduced three industrial engineering tools — process mapping, FMEA, and fault tree analysis — as the foundation for a customized, risk-prioritized quality management (QM) plan for any radiotherapy department.

## Key Facts / Claims

- Published in *Medical Physics*, July 2016; DOI: 10.1118/1.4947547 [tg100-aapm-2016]
- Authors: M. Saiful Huq, Bruce A. Fraass, Peter B. Dunscombe, John P. Gibbons Jr., et al. [tg100-aapm-2016]
- Represents a paradigm shift: from equipment QC to clinical workflow risk analysis [tg100-aapm-2016]
- Three core risk analysis tools recommended: **process mapping**, **FMEA**, and **fault tree analysis (FTA)** [tg100-aapm-2016]
- IMRT is used as the primary worked example throughout the report [tg100-aapm-2016]
- **RPN = Severity × Occurrence × Detectability** (each scored 1–10; RPN range 1–1,000) [tg100-aapm-2016]
- QM plans should be tailored to specific clinical resources and technology — not applied as generic checklists [tg100-aapm-2016]
- The entire clinical team is considered part of the safety system: physicists, dosimetrists, radiation oncologists, radiation therapists, nurses, and administrative staff [tg100-aapm-2016]
- RO-ILS data (>10,000 events) shows treatment planning accounts for ~30% of incidents, treatment delivery ~26% [tg100-aapm-2016]
- Common planning failures: hand-offs, contouring errors, ambiguous physician instructions, rushed timelines [tg100-aapm-2016]
- Implementation barrier: TG-100 was developed primarily by physicists; poor uptake in other disciplines [tg100-aapm-2016]
