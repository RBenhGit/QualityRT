# Regulatory Framework for Radiation Therapy in the USA

**Type:** Topic
**Status:** developing
**Last updated:** 2026-05-25
**Sources:** [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]

## Summary

Regulatory authority over radiation therapy in the United States is distributed across multiple agencies with no single governing body. The NRC (or its Agreement States) regulates radioactive materials (brachytherapy sources, isotopes); the FDA approves radiation-producing machines but states regulate their use; state radiation control programs govern LINAC-based external beam RT. This fragmented structure creates regulatory variation across 50 states, a key gap compared to national systems in other countries. Mandatory incident reporting (NRC medical events) is public record — with no federal confidentiality protection — creating a two-track system alongside voluntary protected reporting via RO-ILS under PSQIA.

## Key Facts / Claims

- **NRC** regulates medical use of **radioactive materials** (byproduct materials) — brachytherapy, I-131, HDR sources; has done so since 1946 [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
- **FDA** approves radiation-producing machines (LINACs, CT simulators) and radiopharmaceuticals; states (not FDA) regulate actual clinical use [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
- **Agreement States Program:** Most U.S. states have signed NRC agreements transferring regulatory authority; NRC retains oversight via periodic reviews [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
- Non-Agreement States, D.C., Puerto Rico, and U.S. territories regulated directly by NRC [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
- **External beam RT (LINAC-based, no radioactive sources)** falls primarily under **state radiation control programs**, not NRC [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
- **50 different state regulatory agencies** govern radiation safety, creating significant inconsistency across the country [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
- **CRCPD (Conference of Radiation Control Program Directors):** Develops Suggested State Regulations (SSRs) to promote inter-state consistency [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
- **NRC Medical Event Reporting (10 CFR 35.3045):** Mandatory public reporting of serious RT incidents involving radioactive materials (wrong patient, wrong site, dose >20% off, unintended tissue damage) [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
- Mandatory NRC/state reports are **public record** — no federal confidentiality protection; unlike RO-ILS (PSQIA-protected) [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
- Two-track system: mandatory public reports (NRC/state) for serious events + voluntary protected reports (RO-ILS) for all events including near-misses [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
- **Accreditation (ACR-ASTRO, TJC, CMS):** Not regulatory but effectively set standards of practice via accreditation requirements, CoPs, and reimbursement conditions [regulatory-framework-rt-usa]
