# Radiotherapy Quality Management

**Type:** Synthesis
**Status:** developing
**Last updated:** 2026-05-24
**Sources:** [radiotherapy-sources]

## Summary
Quality Management (QM) in radiotherapy is a structured, multi-layered system combining proactive risk analysis, independent verification, incident learning, and adherence to international safety guidelines, all aimed at ensuring accurate and safe radiation treatment delivery. The AAPM TG-100 framework is the dominant US standard; WHO and IAEA provide complementary international frameworks.

## Key Facts / Claims
- TG-100 (2016) shifted the field from prescriptive machine-only QA checklists to proactive, workflow-based risk analysis using FMEA and FTA [radiotherapy-sources]
- WHO's *Radiotherapy Risk Profile* emphasizes double-checks, independent dosimetry validation, and robust handover communication as core safety practices [radiotherapy-sources]
- IAEA Safety Reports Series No. 17 analyzes historical major RT accidents and recommends independent calibration, formalized checklists, and SEPAR (Safety Evaluation of Radiation Therapy) [radiotherapy-sources]
- A comprehensive QM program combines **proactive tools** (FMEA, process mapping, FTA) with **reactive tools** (incident learning via RO-ILS) [radiotherapy-sources]
- QM plans should be tailored to each department's specific technology and clinical resources, not applied as generic checklists [radiotherapy-sources]
- Key international bodies shaping RT QM: AAPM, ASTRO, WHO, IAEA [radiotherapy-sources]

## Key Terms Glossary
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **QM (Quality Management)** | The overall program of policies, procedures, and tools ensuring safe, accurate RT |
| **QA (Quality Assurance)** | Machine and process checks that verify equipment and workflows meet specifications |
| **FMEA** | Failure Mode and Effects Analysis — proactive scoring of failure risks (RPN = S×O×D) |
| **FTA** | Fault Tree Analysis — top-down logical mapping of root causes of major failures |
| **RPN** | Risk Priority Number = Severity × Occurrence × Detectability |
| **Process Mapping** | Step-by-step documentation of the full RT workflow as a prerequisite to risk analysis |
| **Just Culture** | A non-punitive reporting environment that encourages disclosure of errors and near misses |
| **Near Miss** | An event that could have caused harm but did not reach the patient |
| **SEPAR** | Safety Evaluation of Radiation Therapy (IAEA framework for proactive safety management) |
| **Independent Dosimetry Validation** | Second-check of dose calculations by a separate physicist or system |
| **PSQIA** | Patient Safety and Quality Improvement Act (2005) — US law protecting incident report confidentiality |
