# Audit, Accreditation & Patterns of Practice in Radiotherapy

**Type:** Topic
**Status:** mature
**Last updated:** 2026-06-18
**Sources:** [ritter-ebrt-audit-2012], [ford-spa-patterns-2015], [schechter-accreditation-survey-2017], [donaldson-quality-standards-2014]

## Summary
Audit and accreditation are the structural mechanisms by which radiation oncology departments verify that their processes conform to the (vast) body of professional-society, regulatory, and best-practice guidance. Self-audit checklists (e.g. a 65-item EBRT audit tool) and standardized self-assessment instruments (the AAPM **Safety Profile Assessment, SPA**) let clinics benchmark themselves, prepare for **ACR-ASTRO / APEx accreditation**, and target the highest-risk gaps. National SPA data reveal a consistent pattern: clinics comply well with *regulated, billable, or long-established* practices (dosimetry calibration, IMRT QA, physics chart checks) but comply *poorly* with newer or "softer" safety practices — near-miss incident reporting, prospective physician peer review, prospective risk assessment (FMEA) of new systems, and independent commissioning review — several of which have been single points of failure in catastrophic misadministrations.

## Key Facts / Claims
- The radiation oncology guidance landscape is enormous — "more than a dozen organizations" and "tens of thousands of pages" (ACR-ASTRO practice guidelines, >100 AAPM Task Group reports, CRCPD state regulations, IAEA/ICRU/NCRP) — making a distilled audit tool valuable [ritter-ebrt-audit-2012]
- A physicist working group built a **65-item EBRT self-audit checklist** (covering IMRT, SBRT, SRS, VMAT); piloted at 3 sites it identified **48 process improvements**; each item is scored by *importance* (1–3) and each gap by *severity* (1–3) to prioritize remediation [ritter-ebrt-audit-2012]
- The tool encodes an 8-element **"vision of a culture of quality"**: living/approved documents, exception management, concordance with recommendations, training & feedback, internal + external peer review, proper acceptance testing/commissioning, dedicated QM staff, and a standing quality committee [ritter-ebrt-audit-2012]
- It is designed as prep for **ACR-ASTRO practice accreditation**, an annual review tool, or a catalyst for QI — especially useful for small / single-physicist clinics [ritter-ebrt-audit-2012]
- The AAPM **Safety Profile Assessment (SPA, spa.aapm.org, launched July 2013)** is an online 92-indicator self-assessment across 4 sections: institutional culture, quality management, managing change/innovation, and clinical performance; it benchmarks against a national database and qualifies for ABR MOC practice quality improvement [ford-spa-patterns-2015]
- Across **114 US clinics** (mean 64 patients/day), the overall mean SPA score was **1.45 on a 5-point scale (1 = best)** — generally positive but with wide variation [ford-spa-patterns-2015]
- **Highest compliance** was for regulated, billable, or strongly recommended items: dosimetry calibration, IMRT patient-specific QA, initial physics plan review, weekly physics chart checks, patient-ID verification, brachytherapy source checks [ford-spa-patterns-2015]
- **Lowest compliance (most concerning)**: clinical staff *submitting* near-miss reports (worst, 2.78) and departments *formally reviewing* near-misses (2.59); physician peer review within the first treatment week (2.43); prospective risk assessment (FMEA) of new clinical systems (2.34); independent review of commissioning results (2.32) — a known single point of failure in serious accidents; time-out at simulation (2.23); regular SOP review (2.25) [ford-spa-patterns-2015]
- **Institutional culture (section I, mean 1.99) scored significantly worse than clinical performance (section IV, 1.54)** — safety culture is the emerging weak area relative to established technical practice [ford-spa-patterns-2015]
- **More than half** of responding clinics lacked formal written policies for several hazardous processes (laterality verification, image-import orientation checks, evaluation of prior-RT impact) — yet SOPs are a cornerstone of high-reliability safety [ford-spa-patterns-2015]

### Quality-standards taxonomy (Donaldson et al. 2014)
- A collation of **454 quality standards across 8 national/international documents** (ACRO, ACR, Canadian CPQR, EC clinical-audit guideline, IAEA QUATRO, UK NCAT, UK NPSA "Towards Safer Radiotherapy," RANZCR) classified them via **Donabedian's structure/process/outcome × target (patient/staff/equipment-process/organization)** [donaldson-quality-standards-2014]
- **64% of standards addressed *structure*, 26% *process*, and only 10% *outcome*** — confirming that RT quality standards are heavily skewed toward structural prerequisites and contain few outcome measures [donaldson-quality-standards-2014]
- Targets were dominated by equipment/clinical-process (41%) and organization (20%) over patient (11%) and staff (17%); Donabedian's model proved a reliable organizing framework (inter-rater κ fair→substantial) [donaldson-quality-standards-2014]

### Accreditation survey-day primer (Schechter 2017)
- A first-person narrative of an ASTRO **APEx / ACR accreditation survey** describes ~2 years and >100 hours of preparation distilling ~800 pages of recommendations [schechter-accreditation-survey-2017]
- Practical enablers: a standing **weekly accreditation subcommittee** of department leaders (QA/safety director, lead physicist/therapist/dosimetrist/nurse, administrators, IT) with action items tracked to completion; a custom **EMR "encounter tab"** to organize chart documents for surveyors; hardcopy **policy binders** and an **electronic physics QA folder**; surveyors are a physicist + physician; the outcome is known 8–12 weeks later [schechter-accreditation-survey-2017]
