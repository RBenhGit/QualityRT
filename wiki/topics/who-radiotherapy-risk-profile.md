# WHO Radiotherapy Risk Profile: Technical Manual

**Type:** Topic
**Status:** developing
**Last updated:** 2026-05-24
**Sources:** [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]

## Summary

The WHO Radiotherapy Risk Profile (2008) is a 53-page technical manual that reviews actual radiotherapy accidents and near-misses from international literature, performing root cause analysis to derive evidence-based safety recommendations. It is the only authoritative source in the field that bases its recommendations primarily on what actually went wrong — rather than on theoretical risk frameworks — making it a retrospective complement to prospective tools like TG-100's FMEA.

## Key Facts / Claims

- Publisher: World Health Organization (WHO), 2008; ISBN: 9789241596954 [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]
- 53 pages; PDF: https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/patient-safety/radiotherapy/090715-who-radiotherapy_risk_profile.pdf [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]
- Analyzed **3,125 errors** and **4,616 quasi-errors (near-misses)** from international incident reports [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]
- **Most common contributing factor:** dosimetry-related errors [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]
- Only source in this field to base recommendations on evidence from actual events via root cause analysis [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]
- Covers the full treatment chain: decision to treat → patient positioning → treatment planning → patient setup → treatment verification [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]
- A comprehensive **Delivery Quality Assurance (DQA)** program is described as **mandatory** for every radiotherapy practice [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]
- QA scope must extend beyond physical/technical aspects to encompass all activities from patient entry through follow-up [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]
- Key safety measures: independent double-checks, independent dosimetry validation, robust handover communication [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]
- Most accidents share common root causes: inadequate independent verification, poor communication at handovers, insufficient checklists, undetected equipment malfunctions [who-radiotherapy-risk-profile-2008]
