# IAEA Safety Reports Series No. 17: Prevention of Accidental Exposure in Radiation Therapy

**Type:** Topic
**Status:** developing
**Last updated:** 2026-05-24
**Sources:** [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]

## Summary

IAEA Safety Reports Series No. 17 (2000) reviews historical radiotherapy accidents to help facilities test their own vulnerability and establish proactive safety practices. It introduces the SEPAR (Safety Evaluation of Radiation Therapy Applied to Routine Practice) framework and emphasizes that most accidents involve multiple contributing failures rather than a single isolated error — making defense-in-depth (multiple safety layers) the essential preventive strategy.

## Key Facts / Claims

- Publisher: International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), Vienna, 2000 [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- Series: Safety Reports Series No. 17 [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- PDF: https://www-pub.iaea.org/mtcd/publications/pdf/pub1084_web.pdf [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- Draws on documented major radiotherapy accidents: San José (Costa Rica), Panama, Bialystok (Poland) [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- Core principle: **accidents can be prevented by applying several safety layers** — defense in depth [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- Most accidents involve **multiple contributing failures**, not a single isolated error [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- Equipment malfunctions alone rarely cause accidents; human and procedural failures are almost always also present [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- **Independent calibration** is mandatory — equipment calibration must be independently verified, not solely by the operator who set it up [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- **Formalized clinical checklists** required at each critical step in the treatment chain [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- Introduces the **SEPAR framework** (Safety Evaluation of Radiation Therapy Applied to Routine Practice) for systematic safety evaluation [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- Absence of safety culture (where errors are not reported for fear of blame) is a recurring systemic risk factor [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
- The Panama accident specifically involved treatment planning system errors leading to patient deaths [iaea-safety-reports-17-2000]
