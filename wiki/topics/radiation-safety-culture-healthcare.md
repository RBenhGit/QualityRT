# Radiation Safety Culture in Healthcare

**Type:** Topic
**Status:** mature
**Last updated:** 2026-06-18
**Sources:** [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks], [kusano-safety-culture-2015], [mazur-eventlearning-cqi-culture-2015], [chera-multifaceted-initiatives-2014]

## Summary

Radiation safety culture in healthcare is the assembly of characteristics and attitudes in an organization — its managers and workers — that ensures safety issues receive priority attention commensurate with their significance. The IAEA operationalizes this through ten Safety Culture Traits adapted from US NRC work, covering individual accountability, questioning attitude, communication, leadership, decision-making, respectful environment, continuous learning, problem resolution, raising concerns, and work processes. Healthcare is unique as the only domain that intentionally exposes humans to radiation, making safety culture especially critical; radiation incidents in medicine cause more early health effects and deaths than any other peaceful use of radiation, including nuclear power.

## Key Facts / Claims

- **IAEA definition:** "The assembly of characteristics and attitudes in the organizations, its managers and workers which assures that, as an overriding priority, safety issues receive the attention warranted by their significance." [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- Radiation incidents in medicine cause **more early acute health effects and deaths than any other peaceful use of radiation**, including nuclear power [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- The **Bonn Call for Action** (IAEA/WHO, 2012) identifies strengthening radiation protection safety culture as one of 10 core actions to improve radiation protection in medicine [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- A **trait** is a personal or organizational pattern of thinking, feeling, and behaving that prioritizes safety [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- The 10 traits were originally developed by the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) and adapted by IAEA for healthcare [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- Healthcare is uniquely positioned: it is the **only field using radiation that intentionally exposes humans** to radiation — framing decision-making with Justification, Optimization, and Limitation [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- **Complacency** is identified as a key contributor to radiation incidents, exemplified by the Bialystok, Poland radiotherapy overexposure accident [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- The framework integrates **Just Culture** principles (personal accountability, respectful environment) and dose-reduction programs (Image Gently®, Image Wisely®) [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- **Organizational climate** with distrust of management reduces personal responsibility for safety and lowers engagement in safety behaviors [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- Safety culture must extend to **all staff levels** — not just clinical leads but also medical radiation technologists, nurses, and administrative staff [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- **Fear of retaliation** is a major barrier to upward safety communication; a Safety Conscious Work Environment (SCWE) policy must protect workers who raise concerns [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]

### Incident learning measurably improves safety culture (Kusano et al. 2015)
- A 2-year prospective study (Univ. of Washington, 1,897 reports) is the **first quantitative demonstration that running a high-volume incident-learning system measurably improves patient safety culture** (validated AHRQ Hospital Survey on Patient Safety Culture; 78–80% response rates at baseline/1yr/2yr) [kusano-safety-culture-2015]
- **Statistically significant, sustained improvements**: belief the department openly discusses ways to prevent errors (66%→86%), confidence that reports are used to improve the system (53%→76%), and that changes are evaluated for effectiveness (46%→64%); *no* culture dimension worsened [kusano-safety-culture-2015]
- **Punitive concerns fell**: worry about embarrassment in front of colleagues (37%→19%) and fear of getting colleagues in trouble (49%→29%); staff "not having reported in the prior year" dropped 57%→29% [kusano-safety-culture-2015]
- The relationship is **reciprocal**: a positive culture is needed to adopt incident learning, and incident learning use then further strengthens the culture; leadership commitment, multidisciplinary weekly review, and prompt feedback are the key enablers [kusano-safety-culture-2015]
- **Communication was the most commonly perceived source of error** by staff; anonymity was optional and used in <2% of reports, reinforcing (not undermining) the no-blame culture [kusano-safety-culture-2015]

### Event learning + continuous quality improvement build culture (Mazur et al. 2015, UNC)
- A "good catch" event-learning program plus a formal **Lean/A3 (Toyota Plan-Do-Study-Act) CQI program** produced improvements across **all 12 AHRQ safety-culture dimensions** (67%→85% total positive, 2009→2013) [mazur-eventlearning-cqi-culture-2015]
- Of 560 good catches, **~64% were performance issues** (not following standardized processes, suboptimal communication), 20% lack of standardization, 16% technological/environmental — consistent with WHO/ROSIS estimates that ~60% of RT incidents stem from suboptimal human performance [mazur-eventlearning-cqi-culture-2015]
- **Safety-barrier "utility" was highest at pretreatment QA checks (physics/dosimetry, ~53%) and first-day therapist checks** — the barriers closest to the patient [mazur-eventlearning-cqi-culture-2015]
- Front-line staff (nurses, therapists) drove most A3 improvement projects; engaging physicians/physicists in CQI was harder — "make better systems: it's the process, not the person" [mazur-eventlearning-cqi-culture-2015]

### Multifaceted operational + safety initiatives (Chera et al. 2014, UNC)
- Five quantified initiatives improved both operations and **all 12 AHRQ safety-culture dimensions** (2009→2011) [chera-multifaceted-initiatives-2014]
- **HDR-brachytherapy nurse workload** (NASA-TLX >55–60, "overwork") was reduced below 55 via workflow and treatment-room-layout (human-factors) changes [chera-multifaceted-initiatives-2014]
- **Therapist interruptions cut from ~4 to <1 per treatment**; CT-sim delays from >50% to <10%; patient wait times ~45% lower; replans cut from 11% to 6% via systematic pretreatment peer review (a daily "morning huddle") [chera-multifaceted-initiatives-2014]
- Pre-RT peer review suggested a **major change in 51% of plans** (vs only 1% major change at the later on-treatment review) — strong support for reviewing *before* planning/treatment [chera-multifaceted-initiatives-2014]
- **Inefficient systems breed workarounds and corner-cutting** that erode standardization and raise risk; physician buy-in/leadership was the biggest obstacle and the biggest enabler [chera-multifaceted-initiatives-2014]

## The Ten IAEA Safety Culture Traits

| # | Trait | Core Sub-characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Individual Responsibility** | High Standards, Teamwork, Job Ownership |
| 2 | **Questioning Attitude** | Radiation sources are special, Avoid complacency, Challenge the unknown, Challenge assumptions |
| 3 | **Effective Safety Communication** | Work process communications, Bases for decisions, Free flow of information, Expectations |
| 4 | **Leadership Responsibility** | Resources, Field presence, Incentives/sanctions/rewards, Strategic commitment to safety, Change management, Roles/responsibilities/authorities |
| 5 | **Decision-Making** | Systematic/rigorous/thorough, Authority, Expectations (conservative under uncertainty) |
| 6 | **Respectful Work Environment** | Respect is evident, High level of trust, Opinions are valued, Conflict resolution |
| 7 | **Continuous Learning** | Operating experience, Training, Self-assessment, Benchmarking |
| 8 | **Problem Identification and Resolution** | Identification, Trending, Resolution, Evaluation |
| 9 | **Environment for Raising Concerns** | SCWE policy, Alternate process for raising concerns |
| 10 | **Work Processes** | Resources, Documentation, Design margins, Procedure adherence |

## Key Incident Examples from the Handbook

- **Interventional radiology (Trait 1):** Patient received cumulative ~12 Gy across prior procedures; no single provider took responsibility to review the full radiation history — resulting in radiation injury [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- **HDR brachytherapy / Bialystok (Trait 2):** Software upgrade bug changed treatment parameters; operator proceeded without questioning error messages, causing mis-positioned source delivery [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- **Nuclear medicine I-131 (Trait 3):** Handwritten prescription for 5.5 GBq misread as 7.5 GBq (the "usual" dose); ambiguous communication and failure to verify with prescriber [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- **CT imaging (Trait 4):** Leadership used unqualified outside technologists without proper training oversight; two pediatric patients scanned with adult protocols [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- **Prostate seed implant (Trait 7):** Seeds implanted at wrong activity (10 vs 18 MBq/seed); similar events persisted at other facilities for over 10 years despite widespread reporting [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- **HDR brachytherapy (Trait 8):** Alarm routinely overridden; broken Ir-192 wire left source in patient, resulting in patient death and exposure of ~12 other people [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]
- **Radiotherapy (Trait 4/8):** Third cobalt-60 shift added by director without RPO consultation, regulatory notification, or staff input; patient identification errors resulted in wrong treatments [radiation-safety-culture-trait-talks]

## How to Build Safety Culture (Practical Steps)

1. **Lead by example** — follow all radiation protection policies; management commitment is visible and demonstrable
2. **Communicate** — weekly/monthly safety talks; clear written procedures accessible to all staff
3. **Prioritize critical activities** — correct patient identification, incident learning system participation, dose optimization
4. **Involve employees** — include frontline staff in identifying challenges and solutions; ensure comprehensive training
