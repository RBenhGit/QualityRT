# Just Culture in Radiotherapy

**Type:** Topic
**Status:** developing
**Last updated:** 2026-07-11
**Sources:** [rt-safety-shield-pptx], [rt-safety-shield-traits-pptx], [davies-aviation-safety-2017], [knoos-lessons-accidents-2017], [marks-swiss-cheese-2015]

## Summary

Just Culture is the foundational philosophy for safety reporting in radiotherapy: an organization that assigns individual blame whenever something goes wrong suppresses reporting, creates fear, and prevents systemic learning. Just Culture occupies the middle ground between No-Blame Culture (too permissive; removes accountability) and Blame Culture (counterproductive; scapegoats individuals while leaving systems unchanged). The goal is to ask "what in the system allowed this to happen?" rather than "who is at fault?", while still holding individuals accountable for deliberate violations.

## Key Facts / Claims

- **Three-tier model** distinguishes: Blame Culture (punishes individuals → fear, under-reporting), No-Blame Culture (removes all accountability → risky behaviour tolerated), and Just Culture (balanced: investigates system causes; does not excuse wilful violations) [rt-safety-shield-pptx]
- "An organization that blames individually when there is a failure prevents learning and creates fear of reporting. Instead we look for the root of the problem, not the person to blame." [rt-safety-shield-pptx]
- Just Culture is one of the four pillars of the ICAO aviation Safety Management System (SMS) — under "safety promotion" — and was adapted into healthcare by James Reason [davies-aviation-safety-2017]
- A blame culture actively *encourages* under-reporting; near-miss events disappear from the data, removing the most numerous substrate for systemic learning [marks-swiss-cheese-2015]
- In RO (Dominello 10-yr Wayne State data): zero nurse-reported and only one physician-reported error in 10 years — a visible symptom of a reporting-culture gap, not a near-miss-free environment [marks-swiss-cheese-2015]
- Just Culture is singled out as a **core lesson from RT accidents**: the Glasgow/Lisa Norris case and others reveal that fear of individual blame caused latent system failures to persist undetected [knoos-lessons-accidents-2017]
- When leadership role-models Just Culture, near-miss reporting rates rise and actual harm incidents decline (Clark 5-yr Ottawa data showed 54% reduction in serious incidents after a high-reporting ILS was established) [davies-aviation-safety-2017]
- Just Culture requires **non-punitive, non-managerial reporting channels** — aviation prefers confidential reports that bypass line management to prevent fear of retribution [davies-aviation-safety-2017]
- "A just culture recognizes that an employee is not responsible for systemic errors outside their control. We don't focus on human errors — we fix systems." [rt-safety-shield-traits-pptx]
- A later version of the departmental deck contrasts **Blame Culture (the past)** — searching for the guilty party, concealment of errors, a fear-driven approach to human error, low trust — against **Just Culture (today)** — advancing despite systemic weaknesses via open reporting tools, a safe environment for learning, and zero tolerance specifically for *repeat, deliberate* violations of established rules (not for honest error) [rt-safety-shield-traits-pptx]
- The deck's closing call-to-action reframes reporting as a positive professional norm rather than an admission of failure: "a near-miss is tomorrow's solution," and filing a report is itself "an act of clinical responsibility" [rt-safety-shield-traits-pptx]

## Three-Component Framework

| Culture Type | Behaviour | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Blame Culture** (תרבות אשמה) | Individual punished for every error | Reporting collapses; system causes persist |
| **Just Culture** (תרבות צדק) | System investigated; wilful violations sanctioned | High reporting; learning and improvement |
| **No-Blame Culture** (תרבות ללא אשמה) | No accountability for anyone | Deliberate risk-taking tolerated; safety deteriorates |
