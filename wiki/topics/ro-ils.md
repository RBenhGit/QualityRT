# RO-ILS: Radiation Oncology Incident Learning System

**Type:** Topic
**Status:** mature
**Last updated:** 2026-06-18
**Sources:** [ro-ils-astro-aapm], [roils-first-year-2015], [evans-ro-ils-disciplines-2015]

## Summary

RO-ILS is the only medical specialty society-sponsored incident learning system in radiation oncology, co-launched by ASTRO and AAPM as part of the Target Safely initiative (2010). It provides a secure, federally protected, non-punitive online portal where U.S. radiation oncology centers can report near-misses and safety incidents. Aggregate national data is reviewed by the Radiation Oncology Healthcare Advisory Council (RO-HAC) and published as case studies, safety alerts, and quarterly benchmarking reports to enable shared learning across the field.

## Key Facts / Claims

- Co-sponsored by ASTRO and AAPM; operations partner is Clarity PSO (a federally recognized Patient Safety Organization) [ro-ils-astro-aapm]
- Part of ASTRO's **Target Safely** patient protection initiative, launched 2010 [ro-ils-astro-aapm]
- **No enrollment fees** for radiation therapy centers [ro-ils-astro-aapm]
- Operates under **PSQIA (Patient Safety and Quality Improvement Act of 2005)** — federal confidentiality and privilege protections apply; data cannot be used in malpractice litigation [ro-ils-astro-aapm]
- Enables a **"just culture"**: non-punitive environment where staff can log incidents without fear of blame [ro-ils-astro-aapm]
- Database contains **over 10,000 reported events** (initial); as of end of 2024: **41,516 events** from **781 enrolled facilities** [ro-ils-astro-aapm], [incident-learning-systems-international]
- 24% of reported events were actual incidents reaching patients; ~10% rated potentially severe or critical [incident-learning-systems-international]
- Over 100 educational releases issued (case studies, safety notices, themed reports, webinars) [incident-learning-systems-international]
- Incident distribution: ~30% in treatment planning, ~26% in treatment delivery [ro-ils-astro-aapm]
- **RO-HAC** (Radiation Oncology Healthcare Advisory Council): 8-member expert body (radiation oncologists, physicists, administrators, dosimetrists) that reviews data and issues alerts and reports [ro-ils-astro-aapm]
- Institutions receive **quarterly benchmarking reports** comparing their safety events to national trends [ro-ils-astro-aapm]
- Qualifies as **MOC Part 4 PQI** (Practice Quality Improvement) activity for ABR maintenance of certification [ro-ils-astro-aapm]

### First-year experience (Hoopes et al. 2015)
- RO-ILS was initiated at the 2010 ASTRO/AAPM "Call to Action" safety meeting in Miami; ASTRO approved the proposal June 2011; beta testing 2013–2014 (14 sites); fully operational 2014 [roils-first-year-2015]
- Its data structure is based directly on the AAPM 2012 consensus report (Ford et al.) [roils-first-year-2015]
- Uses **AHRQ's three event classifications**: *incidents* (reached the patient), *near misses* (close calls, did not reach patient), and *unsafe conditions* (raise probability of an event) [roils-first-year-2015]
- As of Feb 2015: **46 institutions contracted, 33 pending; 27 sites had entered 739 events** (median 14/site, range 1–182); 358 (48%) pushed to the national database [roils-first-year-2015]
- Of 220 nationally-analyzed events (2014): **44% patient incidents, 33% near misses, 23% unsafe conditions** [roils-first-year-2015]
- **Wrong isocenter identification** was a systematic factor in ~15% of first-quarter events — chosen as the inaugural RO-HAC focus topic [roils-first-year-2015]
- **Treatment planning + pretreatment review/verification were the most common error-origination steps (33% of events)** — consistent with treatment preparation being the dominant error point [roils-first-year-2015]
- Nearly **3× as many events involved 3D-CRT as IMRT (29% vs 11%)**, congruent with evidence of lower error rates in IMRT despite IMRT's rising share of EBRT (0%→54% of Medicare cases, 2002–2010) [roils-first-year-2015]
- Initial event entry is designed to take **~1 minute**; supplementary investigation by supervisory staff follows [roils-first-year-2015]
- Major early limitation: **high "unsure/unanswered" rates** (event characterization 59%, workflow step 60%), hobbling aggregate analysis — driving ongoing data-element refinement [roils-first-year-2015]
- One radiation oncology group reported a **>30% reduction in safety events** attributed to incident-analysis interventions [roils-first-year-2015]

### RO-ILS rationale & accreditation role (Evans 2015)
- RO-ILS launched **June 19, 2014**; as of Feb 2015 there were **70 participating sites and >320 events entered** [evans-ro-ils-disciplines-2015]
- The case for an ILS: medical errors are estimated to be the **third leading cause of death** in hospitalized patients, and RT incidents occur at ~**0.7% per treatment, or roughly one near-miss per patient treatment course** — so incidents happen in every clinic [evans-ro-ils-disciplines-2015]
- The PSQIA confidentiality/privilege protections were tested and **upheld in federal court (Tinal v. Norton Healthcare, 2014)** — reports remained undiscoverable even when sought for a (non-malpractice) employment case [evans-ro-ils-disciplines-2015]
- Why errors matter clinically: across multiple cooperative-group trials, **RT protocol deviations are consistently associated with decreased survival** (e.g., NSCLC ~13% survival decrement; head & neck HR 1.99; pancreas HR 1.336; meta-analysis HR 1.74) — proper delivery is critical to outcome [evans-ro-ils-disciplines-2015]
- Participating in RO-ILS (or another PSO) **satisfies ASTRO practice-accreditation Standard 7 (Culture of Safety)**, is referenced in ACR accreditation requirements, and counts as an ABR MOC PQI project [evans-ro-ils-disciplines-2015]
