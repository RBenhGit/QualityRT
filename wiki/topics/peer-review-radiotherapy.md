# Peer Review in Radiation Oncology

**Type:** Topic
**Status:** mature
**Last updated:** 2026-06-18
**Sources:** [marks-peer-review-2013], [cox-contouring-rounds-2015], [matuszak-sbrt-peer-review-2016]

## Summary
Peer review ("audit and feedback") is one of the most effective tools for assuring the quality of the *qualitative, patient-specific* medical decisions in radiation therapy — the choices (target delineation, dose/fractionation, plan acceptability) that lack a single objectively "correct" answer and so are not amenable to ordinary process control. The foundational guidance is the 2013 ASTRO Target Safely white paper (Marks et al.), which catalogs and prioritizes the steps along the RT workflow that most benefit from peer review and recommends embedding it in routine practice within an open, just culture. Traditional vehicles are weekly **chart rounds** and **prospective pretreatment tumor boards**; the field is moving toward earlier, pre-planning review (especially of contours) where it has the greatest leverage.

## Key Facts / Claims
- Peer review applies to **professional qualitative decisions** (e.g., dose-volume prescription), distinct from **process/quality control** of quantitative technical tasks (e.g., machine calibration) [marks-peer-review-2013]
- The 2013 ASTRO white paper was approved by the ASTRO Board (Sept 11, 2012) and **endorsed by AAPM, AAMD (dosimetrists), ASRT (technologists), and accepted by the ACR Commission on Radiation Oncology** [marks-peer-review-2013]
- **Chart rounds** and **prospective pretreatment tumor boards** are the two traditional forms; routine chart-round peer review occurs in essentially all North American centers with accredited residency programs [marks-peer-review-2013]
- **Brundage et al.:** real-time pretreatment review of 3,052 plans over 8 years recommended plan modifications in **~8% of cases** (most common: planning target volumes 31%, critical-structure protection 15%, treatment-volume selection 11%, dose selection 11%, dose distribution 6%) [marks-peer-review-2013]
- **Boxer et al.:** real-time peer-review audit changed management in 8/208 patients (~4%); a posttreatment audit found ~5% of cases had controversial/concerning medical decisions [marks-peer-review-2013]
- **Survey of 57/71 North American academic centers:** protected time + attendance monitoring improved senior-faculty participation; routine EBRT cases reviewed at 80% of institutions vs radiosurgery 58% and brachytherapy 40–47%; median **2.7 minutes per patient**; major changes (dose/plan) occurred in <10% of cases at 75% of centers [marks-peer-review-2013]
- **Seven prioritized targets** for peer review, ranked Level 1 (highest, where interpatient variation is greatest) to Level 3: **target definition/contouring = Level 1**; first-day treatment-delivery setup = Level 1 (curative/IMRT/SBRT); decision to treat, planning directive, technical plan quality = Level 2; general treatment approach and normal-tissue segmentation = Level 3 (guidelines/atlases exist) [marks-peer-review-2013]
- **Target (contour) delineation is the single most critical peer-review target** because mistargeting causes poor clinical outcomes and varies markedly between patients; it should ideally be reviewed *before* treatment planning begins [marks-peer-review-2013]
- A Johns Hopkins **FMEA** (Ford et al. 2009) flagged the highest-risk tasks as wrong isocenter, wrong markings, incorrect patient in the record-and-verify system, incorrect contours, and wrong CT-sim data — but many real clinic events were not predicted by FMEA [marks-peer-review-2013]
- Peer review must occur in an **open, just culture** (honest mistakes are not punished), but staff *should* be accountable for failing to participate; small practices are encouraged to form **inter-practice peer-review relationships** [marks-peer-review-2013]

### Prospective contouring rounds (Cox et al. 2015)
- **Prospective contouring rounds (CR)** move peer review *earlier*: contours and the written directive are reviewed by a daily multidisciplinary group **after contouring but before treatment planning begins**; planning does not start until CR approval [cox-contouring-rounds-2015]
- Of 581 consecutive conventional EBRT plans, **64% passed on time with no correction; 36% required modification before planning** — most commonly incomplete volumes (11%), prescription/written-directive inconsistencies (9%), and target-volume changes (9%) [cox-contouring-rounds-2015]
- Timely, errorless cases rose **59% → 65% → 70%** over the first 6 months as the program matured (~8 min/case, ~7 cases/day) [cox-contouring-rounds-2015]
- Reviewing before planning **avoids delivering suboptimal fractions and wasteful replanning** — a key advantage over traditional weekly chart rounds, which occur after planning is complete; surveys show only ~1/3 of patients get prospective review yet ~90% of oncologists report changing plans due to peer review [cox-contouring-rounds-2015]
- **Contouring and prescription are among the highest patient-safety-risk steps** in planning; a meta-analysis (Ohri et al.) found RT protocol deviations associated with a **75% increase in treatment-failure and mortality risk** [cox-contouring-rounds-2015]
- MD Anderson's post-completion peer review changed 12% of cases (target-volume change in 69% of modified cases); rates fell over 4 years and authors urged peer review become a national requirement [cox-contouring-rounds-2015]

### Preplanning peer review for SBRT (Matuszak et al. 2016)
- For 513 SBRT cases over 26 months (Univ. of Michigan), preplanning peer review by an independent physician + physicist + dosimetrist produced **at least one change in 22.6% of cases** — potentially preventing suboptimal high-dose-per-fraction treatments [matuszak-sbrt-peer-review-2016]
- Change rates by element: **GTV 8.2%, OAR goals/priorities 7.2%, PTV 5.5%, prescription 4.9%, image registration 2.7%** [matuszak-sbrt-peer-review-2016]
- **Change odds were lower for high-volume sites (lung/liver) and for experienced oncologists** (≥5 prior SBRT cases: OR 0.51) — arguing peer review is *most* valuable at low-volume centers and for uncommon/re-treatment cases (prior RT >5 cm away: OR 2.49) [matuszak-sbrt-peer-review-2016]
- SBRT preplanning review took ~5–20 min/case vs ~2 min/case in weekly chart rounds; the short 1–5 fraction course makes *post-planning* review too late to act on [matuszak-sbrt-peer-review-2016]

## Connections
- [[rt-quality-management]] — peer review is the QA mechanism for the *qualitative/medical* half of a QM program; process control + QA covers the quantitative/technical half
- [[fmea-radiotherapy]] — FMEA is used to prioritize which steps most need peer review (highest-risk, lowest downstream detectability)
- [[incident-learning-systems-international]] — peer review (proactive, pre-treatment) complements incident learning (reactive); oncologist peer review and chart check appear as "safety barriers" in ILS process maps and taxonomies
- [[tg100-aapm]] — TG-100's risk-based QM framework motivates targeting peer-review effort by risk
- [[radiation-safety-culture-healthcare]] — effective peer review depends on an open, just culture where questioning each other is normalized
- [[human-factors-rt-safety]] — "second set of eyes," timeouts, and multi-therapist checks are human-factors defenses embedded in delivery peer review
- [[advanced-modality-rt-safety]] — preplanning peer review is especially high-impact for SBRT/IMRT; the IMRT white paper defers peer-review detail to this paper
- [[audit-accreditation-radiotherapy]] — prospective physician peer review is one of the lowest-compliance SPA safety indicators despite strong ASTRO recommendation
- [[brachytherapy-hdr-safety]] — the ASTRO HDR white paper cites peer review (and ChartRounds) as a defense against task-execution failures

## Open Questions
- What is the measurable impact of peer review on patient *outcomes* (vs. process changes)? The white paper calls for efficacy studies.
- How should peer review adapt to adaptive/online-replanning and AI-assisted contouring workflows?
- What software/vendor tools best integrate peer review into the treatment planning system?
